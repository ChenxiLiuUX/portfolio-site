Nonprofit legal tech, 2026

Maya Migrate

A calm, free case-management app for people in a frightening process.

Role
Lead product designer
Team
Founders, engineers
Timeline
Year, e.g. 2025–2026
Problem
Immigrants and the solo lawyers who serve them needed a free, trustworthy way to manage cases. The product started as engineer-made mockups, with no design system and a critical safety feature described in technical jargon.
Outcome
A design system, a redesigned app and landing page, and an emergency check-in feature, designed end to end and now live.

Context

Maya Migrate is an immigrant-led nonprofit. Its platform is free for immigrants and the solo lawyers who represent them, it keeps data collection lean, and it never sells user data. The people using it are often under real stress, so every screen has to feel steady and clear.

I joined as the only designer. My first step was a UX critique of the existing mockups, followed by redesigns of the core screens.

Nothing looked like it belonged together

Screens built directly by engineers had no shared colors, type, or spacing, so the app felt unfinished at the moments people most needed to trust it.Known going in, from the UX critique

The safety feature spoke in jargon

The emergency feature was called a "dead man's switch" that users would "arm," language that is confusing at best and alarming for someone worried about detention.Known going in, from the project scope

Key decisions

Trauma-informed design was the brief. In practice, that came down to four decisions.

Plain words for a frightening feature

The situationThe feature alerts a person's trusted contacts and lawyer if they stop checking in, which could mean they've been detained.

What I choseI renamed it the check-in feature and replaced "arm" with "turn on." I kept the copy calm and factual throughout, describing what will happen without dramatizing it.

Make every color mean one thing

The situationIn an app about legal risk, a stray red or a warning-colored button can read as an emergency.

What I choseIndigo is the primary surface, amber means paused or needs attention, red is reserved for destructive actions only, and green signals status. I checked contrast across the palette and flagged pairings that didn't pass.

Build the system before the screens

The situationAs the only designer, I needed every new screen to be fast to make and consistent with the rest.

What I choseI set up five color ramps, text styles, a spacing scale, and corner-radius tokens, then later ran a cleanup pass that bound every frame to the shared styles so the file stays maintainable for whoever comes next.

Design the alert as a whole journey

The situationA safety feature fails if any step is unclear: setting it up, living with it, or receiving the alert.

What I choseI designed the full path: a setup wizard for desktop and mobile, dashboard states for empty, active, and paused, and five email templates for the people who receive alerts.

The design

Beyond the app itself, I designed the landing page, an animated splash screen in the brand indigo, and a research plan for testing the check-in feature with real users.
